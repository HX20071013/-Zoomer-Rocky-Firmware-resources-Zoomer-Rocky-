This is the second part of the Paw Patrol Zoomer Rocky re-development project, featuring firmware resources.
I performed a hardware analysis of the original motherboard. In addition to the COB mud chip mentioned in the previous module, the original motherboard includes two H-bridge motor drive chips, each of which manages one motor.
However, what truly matters is a 25-series SPI FLASH with a capacity of 0.5 GB. This is a substantial storage container housing all of Zoomer Rocky’s pre-set resources. Among these resources, the most significant are the audio resources marked as 150+ on the packaging box.
With these resources, you can build your own RVC voice transformation model, which can also be used to develop new movement systems. This is, in my opinion, the most important aspect of the entire system.
However, it is regrettable that, for reasons unknown to me, whether due to resource conservation or other design objectives, Zoomer Rocky, although it has a dance feature, features two dance tracks that are only about 20 seconds long each. In my opinion, this is an essential piece of information that urgently needs to be supplemented for the development of the new system. Additionally, my hardware resources are more abundant: the STM32F103RET6 itself has storage capacity and can store portions or even the entire programs of modules such as motion control, micro-switch detection, volume switch detection, and language switch detection. The SPI Flash can not only continue to use the official 0.5GB but can also utilize larger capacities. Furthermore, since both the official and my projects use standard storage devices, different models can be substituted.
Therefore, in order to supplement the dance functions and utilize complete resources, I consulted the official promotional documents and discovered that the track information for two songs—\"Paw Patrol\" and \"Pup Pup Boogie\"—was already included in the promotional materials. Leveraging China's extensive MAD community, I successfully located the full resources of these two tracks on Bilibili and converted them into the .wav format, which is best supported by the STM32F1 series, using cloudconvert before uploading them together.
The firmware information has not been analyzed and includes data on actions, detection, and other details. Therefore, when analyzing it, please be aware that the extracted audio files will contain audio in both English and French, as well as Rocky’s dog barks. For training RVC, be sure to avoid importing dog bark-related resources into the training model to prevent unwanted mixing and noise.
Since Rocky's vocalizations already encompass various emotions and states, I believe my project does not need to increase the quantity of these resources. Therefore, I do not intend to perform any secondary processing on these dog vocalization assets. If any replicators wish to add animal sound resources, please extend them in your own way. If possible, share your toolchain below this project to assist more people!
